## Rulings

### 1. The stats band: no band

Remove the four counter band from every variant. Attach each number once, inside the sentence or caption of the work that earned it, as static text, following Brittany's per project metric placement without taking her layout. Groovy's 16M+ and $1M+ are past tense with their years. Hydra's 6.1M is current, so it carries an as of month. Reasons: the preview intro already states 16M+ and 6.1M in prose, the band repeats them within one screen (observed, capture and HTML), four animated counters before any work is SaaS landing grammar none of the final set uses, and the current counter markup duplicates its own text in extracted HTML, which is a screen reader risk. Grok's "one band of four, then stop" still spends the hero on a repetition. One exception: the story route may keep its large figure, because there the year ruler ties the number to a year.

### 2. The memorable detail: Default and Long on the homepage, the year ruler on /story

Arc's rule is one memorable detail per page, so this is two pages with one each, not two details competing. The homepage detail is Lee Robinson's bio toggle. It is observed working on a live site, it serves the audience order directly (short for clients, long for peers), it works by touch and keyboard, motion is a crossfade with an obvious reduced motion branch, and it costs days, not weeks. The /story detail is the existing 2018 to 2026 year ruler, which ties 16M+ to 2018 and is the only preview interaction no other engineer could ship unchanged.

Rejected: a command palette, because the site has a handful of destinations and a keyboard only feature is invisible to the client audience (Sol is right). Hover image previews, because they are pointer only and, on Dennis's site, recalled rather than observed. A live Hydra server count as the detail, because no seat verified that a public endpoint exists; ship the dated static number, and only go live later if a real endpoint exists with a dated fallback, never animated to fake liveness. A Discord slash command playground on the homepage, because it is the largest build on the table and risks becoming the toy archetype all seats refused; if it is ever built it belongs inside the Hydra case study, not in this pass. Inline project pills are adopted, but they are typography, not a memorable detail. The Kufstein clock and weather demote to one small footer line. The theme switch is standard Arc chrome, not the detail.

### 3. Brittany Chiang: out of the set, pattern absorbed, look refused

Sol and the Muse seat put her top three for the sticky identity and dated rows, and the layout is genuinely good information architecture. I still cut her. The two pane sticky shell conflicts with the single column base every seat agreed on, and it collapses to one column on mobile anyway, so below desktop widths the purchase is mostly dated rows, which Jakub and Benji supply. The captured site is dark only, background rgb(15, 23, 42) in both schemes (observed, capture report), which forfeits Arc's light and dark token story, and Sol's source inspection found a 600px cursor spotlight glow, which Arc bans outright. The Muse seat's own warning is decisive: her site lists the template's star and fork counts, and a layout that forked reads as a template no matter how well Oskar executes it, which is the opposite signal for someone selling eight years of ownership. Keep three functions and credit them to the set instead: date range plus role plus one outcome sentence per row, metric attached to its project, and an in flow "Read my CV" link. If the homepage ever grows past roughly three screens of experience, revisit a sticky identity rail as Arc native components, not as her composition.

### 4. Where Story goes: its own route at /story

Story becomes a secondary route, and the homepage takes its headline plus a slim dated index that links to it. Reasons: the client audience needs current work, availability shape and contact before chronology; the preview story page is 2937px tall and then repeats the same experience list under "The details" (observed, HTML and capture reports), so as a homepage chapter it duplicates the Experience log; and the founding arc is strong enough to deserve a URL peers can share. The homepage keeps "I build software for millions of Discord servers, and have since I was 16." as its headline, since all seats agree it is the best line on the site, and the five chapters compress to five one line year entries (2018 Groovy, 2020 Hydra, 2021 SoundCloud and the shutdown, 2022 Netflix, 2026 three contracts) ending in a link to /story. The year ruler, full chapters and a closing contact block live on /story.
